A two-line explanation of an everyday thing for a 10-year-old. The program writes one entry to `/output/entry.txt`:

> A queue is a caterpillar made of people, shuffling forward one tiny step at a time until its head reaches the front and peels away.
> The caterpillar fits because both move slowly in a single line, but it leaves out that each person in a queue chose to be there and can step out, while a caterpillar's legs have no choice.

## Why this entry

- **It really explains an everyday thing to a 10-year-old.** A queue is something every child has stood in; the entry says what it is and how it moves, in words a child follows.
- **The analogy is concrete and surprising.** A caterpillar is something a 10-year-old can see, hold and watch crawl — and a queue as a caterpillar is a picture nobody has met before.
- **Line 1 is one vivid sentence.** One metaphor, no list of comparisons, the picture lands whole: shuffling forward, head reaching the front, peeling away.
- **Line 2 is honest and specific about the limits.** It names exactly what the analogy maps (slow movement in a single line) and exactly what it leaves out (choice: people can step out, a caterpillar's legs cannot).
- **The two lines work as a pair.** The second explains the first rather than repeating it, and never talks down to the reader.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
